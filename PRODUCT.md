# Product

## Register

product

## Users

GuildOS serves fraternity chapter officers, administrators, and members who need one operational source of truth for chapter life. Officers use it during recurring chapter workflows: planning events, opening attendance check-ins, reviewing excuses, issuing fines, managing treasury records, tracking tasks, sending announcements, monitoring standing, referencing bylaws, and assigning roles. Members use it to see obligations clearly, check in from mobile, submit excuses, and understand their own fines or standing without relying on side channels.

## Product Purpose

GuildOS exists to save chapter leaders time, make chapter operations transparent, and log the truth of what happened. Success means routine chapter work is faster, less ambiguous, and more accountable: events and attendance are recorded accurately, financial consequences are traceable, members can see what applies to them, and officers can optimize operations from reliable dashboards instead of scattered messages, spreadsheets, or memory.

## Brand Personality

Sleek, minimalistic, and easy to use. The product should feel operationally serious without being cold: fast to scan, trustworthy under pressure, and direct about status, ownership, and next actions.

## Anti-references

Avoid fraternity-party visual tropes, school-spirit clutter, opaque admin panels, spreadsheet sprawl, and decorative dashboards that look impressive but hide the real state of operations. Avoid playful or mascot-like UI, overbuilt enterprise density, and any design pattern that makes logged facts feel negotiable.

## Design Principles

- Truth before polish: status, timestamps, money, attendance, and permissions must be easy to verify.
- Time saved is the product: frequent workflows should be obvious, short, and forgiving on mobile.
- Transparency earns trust: members and officers should be able to understand what happened, why it matters, and what comes next.
- Dashboards should direct action: large summary surfaces are useful only when they expose operational priorities and drill into real records.
- Role clarity matters: officer-only power, member self-service, and admin access should be visibly distinct without fragmenting the experience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support mobile views for every surface, including dashboard pages that scroll naturally instead of trapping content. Maintain readable contrast across the dark blue UI, visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion-safe interactions, and clear semantic states for success, warning, danger, loading, error, empty, and disabled conditions.
